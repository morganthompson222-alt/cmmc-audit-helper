FCI vs CUI — Know the Difference

FCI: Information provided by the government under a contract. Invoices, payment data, contract terms. Not sensitive. Not marked.

CUI: Information the government creates or possesses that requires safeguarding. Technical drawings, engineering specs, source code. Often marked "CUI" or "Controlled".

Quick test for a contractor: "Does your contract mention DFARS 252.204-7012?" If yes → Level 2.

3. SPRS Score — The Real Formula

The score is not a simple percentage. The DoD methodology:

  • Start at 110 (all requirements met)
  • Subtract deductions for each unmet NIST 800-171 requirement
  • Each control has a weight of 1, 3, or 5 points
  • Submission goes into SPRS (Supplier Performance Risk System)

Control Weight Categories (NIST 800-171)

WeightMeaningExample controls
1 pointBasic safeguardingTraining records, policies documented
3 pointsTechnical controlsSession lock, audit logging, access reviews
5 pointsCritical securityMFA, encryption at rest/transit, incident response plan tested

4. DFARS Clauses You Must Know

ClauseWhat it requires
252.204-7012Safeguard CUI using NIST 800-171. Report cyber incidents to DoD within 72 hours. Flow down to subcontractors.
252.204-7019Must have a current NIST 800-171 self-assessment score in SPRS. Must renew at least every 3 years (every year for basic assessments).
252.204-7020DoD can request to see your assessment and supporting evidence. You must provide it within 14 days.
252.204-7021CMMC certification requirement. This clause drives the C3PAO requirement — currently suspended under Phase II suspension.

5. Evidence — What They Actually Need

For every control, auditors look for three layers of evidence:

Layer 1 — Policy

"We have a rule that says we do this."

  • Written policy document, signed by leadership, dated within last year
  • Example: "Access Control Policy v2.1, signed by CEO, 3/2026"

Layer 2 — Implementation

"Here's proof the rule is actually configured."

  • Screenshots of settings, GPOs, firewall rules, MFA configuration screens
  • Example: Screenshot of Windows password policy GPO showing 14-char minimum

Layer 3 — Operation

"We actively maintain this."

  • Logs, review records, audit trails, training completion certificates
  • Example: Quarterly access review spreadsheet with sign-offs, dated last 12 months
Most common failure: Policy documents exist but zero implementation or operational evidence. "We have a policy" = not enough.

6. The 14 NIST 800-171 Control Families

  1. Access Control (AC) — Who can access what, least privilege, session locks, remote access
  2. Awareness & Training (AT) — Security awareness training for all users, role-based training
  3. Audit & Accountability (AU) — Logging, log review, log protection, alerting
  4. Configuration Management (CM) — Baselines, change control, patching, least functionality
  5. Identification & Authentication (IA) — Unique IDs, MFA, password policies, account lockout
  6. Incident Response (IR) — IR plan, testing, reporting, post-incident review
  7. Maintenance (MA) — Scheduled maintenance, vendor access control, maintenance records
  8. Media Protection (MP) — Media sanitization, labeling CUI, encryption on removable media
  9. Personnel Security (PS) — Background checks, termination procedures, access reviews
  10. Physical Protection (PE) — Physical access controls, visitor logs, environmental controls
  11. Risk Assessment (RA) — Risk assessments, risk register, periodic updates
  12. Security Assessment (CA) — Control testing, vulnerability scanning, POA&M tracking
  13. System & Communications Protection (SC) — Encryption, network segmentation, firewalls, boundary defense
  14. System & Information Integrity (SI) — Antivirus, malware protection, integrity monitoring, flaw remediation

7. The Three Statuses That Matter

StatusWhat it meansSPRS Impact
Implemented Control is fully in place with policy + implementation + operational evidence No deduction
Not Implemented Control is absent or only partially done Deduction applies
Not Applicable Control doesn't apply to the contractor's environment (must justify) No deduction (but must document why)
N/A is not a free pass. You can't just mark controls N/A without justification. A government assessor will challenge N/A claims. Document specific reasons.

8. The Legal Risk – False Claims Act

The False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729-3733) makes it illegal to knowingly submit false claims to the government. This includes:

  • Submitting a SPRS score that doesn't reflect actual implementation
  • Claiming controls are "implemented" when they only exist on paper
  • Falsely marking controls as N/A to boost a score

Penalties: 3x the government's damages + $13,946–$27,894 per false claim (2026 adjusted). Plus debarment from federal contracting.

Recent context: DOJ has been increasingly active on cybersecurity False Claims Act cases since 2021. The Civil Cyber-Fraud Initiative specifically targets contractors who misrepresent cybersecurity compliance.

What you DO:
